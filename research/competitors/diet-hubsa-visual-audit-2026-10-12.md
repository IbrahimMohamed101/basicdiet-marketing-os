## 2. Eight transferable communication mechanisms (not templates)

1. **One hero dish + a 3–6 word headline.** Visible product-led posters make the meal recognizable before reading body copy. Basic Diet adaptation: one **real currently offered photographed** dish, large crop, restrained cream field, short headline.
2. **A concrete everyday context.** Gym mats, working days, outings and travel put the meal in an understandable moment. Basic Diet: link a verified meal photo to a familiar workday or post-gym question; no performance/weight-loss promises.
3. **An instantly recognized object as metaphor.** Clock, map marker, target, scale or simple exercise prop reduces explanation time. Basic Diet: one prop and one benefit question; no unverified delivery-time/weight claim.
4. **Packaging or delivery as visual anchor.** Branded bags, courier vehicle and food boxes help explain the service. Basic Diet: use the restaurant's **actual** packaging/courier imagery only when pixel/rights checked; avoid inventing vehicles.
5. **App screen as product demonstration.** Several thumbnails foreground phone mockups and purchase/choice paths. Basic Diet: native screenshot of the real app (redact personal data) showing verified meal/portion options; never draw fictitious checkout UI.
6. **Question-led fitness interaction.** Food near dumbbells, a bag or trainer creates an easy question rather than a medical statement. Basic Diet: visual poll such as 'بعد التمرين، وش تفضّل؟' with actual available choices.
7. **Seasonal Saudi relevance.** Ramadan, National Day and local settings supply cultural context. Basic Diet: original seasonal content with correct calendar dates and owner-approved offer; never recycle old dates/codes.
8. **Appetite versus information, alternated.** Some thumbnails are almost all food, others have a headline and illustration or offer. Basic Diet: rotate native visual families without turning every post into either a poster wall or pure offer.

## 3. Pitfalls observable in this sample

- Some thumbnails have many text tiers and a very small footer: avoid illegible mobile-sized text.
- Hyper-saturated color and frequent dramatic effects can distract from appetizing, credible real food: **Basic Diet's own** cream/green/orange system takes priority.
- Hyper-stylized characters or celebrity/fictional-character likenesses are unsuitable for direct adaptation without rights; no copying of distinctive composite scenes.
- Visible offer pricing, comparative nutrition, and delivery claims are **the competitor's content only**; never transplant those facts or assert they performed well.
- A designed thumbnail is not proof of an actual motion-based Reel; do not infer hooks, animation cadence or retention from this grid alone.
